How to order a coffee in British English: phrases and a real café conversation

You're at Callum's café. Order a drink to take away and pay.

What you need to do

  1. Order a drink
  2. Say eat in or take away
  3. Pay and say thank you

The conversation

They say

Hiya, what can I get you?

Tip: Ask for your drink with 'Can I have …, please?'

  • Best reply
    Can I have a flat white, please?
    Perfect: polite and clear.
  • Understood, but not natural
    I want coffee.
    He understands, but it sounds blunt. 'Can I have …, please?' is friendlier.
  • Doesn't answer
    Yes, I'm fine, thank you.
    That answers 'How are you?'. He asked what you'd like.
They say

Sure. Is that to eat in or take away?

Tip: It's a choice: say 'eat in' or 'take away'.

  • Best reply
    Take away, please.
    Spot on.
  • Understood, but not natural
    To go, please.
    He'll understand, but in the UK people usually say 'take away'.
  • Doesn't answer
    Yes, please.
    It's an either/or question, so 'yes' doesn't answer it. Pick one.
They say

That's three pounds twenty. Card or cash?

Tip: Say how you want to pay.

  • Best reply
    Card, please.
    Great. Then just tap your card on the reader.
  • Understood, but not natural
    Card.
    Fine. Adding 'please' sounds friendlier.
  • Doesn't answer
    Three pounds, please.
    He told you the price. Now say card or cash.
They say

Lovely. There you go.

Tip: Take your drink and thank him.

  • Best reply
    Thanks very much. Cheers!
    Very British: 'Cheers' is a friendly thank you.
  • Understood, but not natural
    OK.
    Fine, but a thank you is expected here.
  • Doesn't answer
    Sorry, sorry.
    No need to apologise. Just say thank you.

How it ends

No worries. Have a good day!
