Life in the UK test practice: Values and principles (free questions with answers)

This is free practice made by New Life UK. It is not the official test or handbook and does not guarantee a pass. Study the official handbook and book the test only on GOV.UK.

The test has 24 questions, you have 45 minutes, and you need 75% to pass (at least 18 correct).

British life is built on shared values: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of people with different faiths and beliefs, and taking part in community life.

Everyone who lives here is expected to respect and obey the law, respect the rights of others, treat people fairly, look after themselves and their family, and look after the area they live in.

The United Kingdom is England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Great Britain means just England, Scotland and Wales. The Isle of Man and the Channel Islands are Crown dependencies, not part of the UK.

The Life in the UK test has 24 questions and you have 45 minutes. You need 75 percent to pass, so at least 18 correct answers. Most people applying to settle or become citizens also need to show English at B1 level or above.

Key facts

  • The UK: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • Great Britain: England, Scotland and Wales
  • Crown dependencies: The Isle of Man and the Channel Islands: linked to the UK but not part of it
  • Fundamental values: Democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance, community participation
  • The test: 24 questions, 45 minutes, 75% to pass
  • Union Flag: The crosses of St George, St Andrew and St Patrick
  • St David's Day: 1 March, Wales
  • St Andrew's Day: 30 November, Scotland
  • St George's Day: 23 April, England
  • St Patrick's Day: 17 March, Northern Ireland

Practice questions

Which of these is one of the fundamental values of British life?

  • The rule of law
  • Loyalty to a single political party
  • Keeping away from community life
  • Following only religious law
Answer

The rule of law. The rule of law means everyone, including the government, must follow the law.

Which countries make up Great Britain?

  • England, Scotland and Wales
  • England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • England and Wales only
  • England, Scotland and Northern Ireland
Answer

England, Scotland and Wales. Great Britain is the island of England, Scotland and Wales. The UK adds Northern Ireland.

Which countries make up the United Kingdom?

  • England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • England, Scotland and Wales
  • England, Wales and Ireland
  • England, Scotland, Wales and the Isle of Man
Answer

England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The UK is four countries: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

The Isle of Man is part of the United Kingdom. True or false?

  • FALSE
  • TRUE
Answer

FALSE. The Isle of Man is a Crown dependency with its own government. It is not part of the UK.

What are the Channel Islands?

  • Crown dependencies with their own governments
  • Part of England
  • A county of Wales
  • Part of Northern Ireland
Answer

Crown dependencies with their own governments. Jersey and Guernsey are Crown dependencies, like the Isle of Man.

Which of these is a British overseas territory?

  • The Falkland Islands
  • The Isle of Wight
  • Jersey
  • Anglesey
Answer

The Falkland Islands. The Falkland Islands are a British overseas territory in the South Atlantic.

Which is a responsibility of everyone living in the UK?

  • To respect and obey the law
  • To join a political party
  • To go to a place of worship every week
  • To vote for the governing party
Answer

To respect and obey the law. Respecting and obeying the law is expected of everyone who lives here.

Tolerance of people with different faiths and beliefs is a fundamental British value. True or false?

  • TRUE
  • FALSE
Answer

TRUE. Tolerance of different faiths and beliefs is one of the shared values of British life.

Which of these is an example of taking part in community life?

  • Volunteering for a local charity
  • Refusing to speak to neighbours
  • Dropping litter in the park
  • Ignoring local elections
Answer

Volunteering for a local charity. Volunteering, helping neighbours and joining local groups are all ways to take part.

How many questions are there in the Life in the UK test?

  • 24
  • 20
  • 30
  • 50
Answer

24. The test has 24 questions.

How long do you have to answer the questions in the Life in the UK test?

  • 45 minutes
  • 30 minutes
  • 1 hour
  • 2 hours
Answer

45 minutes. You have 45 minutes to answer the 24 questions.

What score do you need to pass the Life in the UK test?

  • 75%
  • 50%
  • 60%
  • 90%
Answer

75%. You need 75 percent, which is 18 out of 24.

What level of English do most people need to show when applying to settle or become a citizen?

  • B1 or above
  • A1
  • A2
  • Native-speaker level only
Answer

B1 or above. The usual requirement is speaking and listening at B1 level or above, unless you are exempt.

What do new citizens do at a citizenship ceremony?

  • Make an oath or affirmation and a pledge
  • Take a driving test
  • Vote in an election
  • Sit the Life in the UK test again
Answer

Make an oath or affirmation and a pledge. New citizens swear an oath or make an affirmation of allegiance to the King and make a pledge to respect the UK's rights and freedoms.

Which country's patron saint is NOT shown on the Union Flag?

  • Wales
  • England
  • Scotland
  • Ireland
Answer

Wales. The Union Flag combines the crosses of St George, St Andrew and St Patrick. Wales has its own flag with a red dragon.

What does the cross of St Andrew look like?

  • A white diagonal cross on a blue background
  • A red cross on a white background
  • A red diagonal cross on a white background
  • A red dragon on green and white
Answer

A white diagonal cross on a blue background. St Andrew's cross, the flag of Scotland, is a white diagonal cross on blue.

When is St David's Day, the national day of Wales?

  • 1 March
  • 17 March
  • 23 April
  • 30 November
Answer

1 March. St David's Day is 1 March.

Who is the patron saint of England?

  • St George
  • St Andrew
  • St David
  • St Patrick
Answer

St George. St George is the patron saint of England; his day is 23 April.

When is St Andrew's Day?

  • 30 November
  • 1 March
  • 17 March
  • 23 April
Answer

30 November. St Andrew's Day, Scotland's national day, is 30 November.

In the UK, it is acceptable to treat someone unfairly because of their religion. True or false?

  • FALSE
  • TRUE
Answer

FALSE. Treating people unfairly because of their religion is against British values and, in many situations, against the law.
