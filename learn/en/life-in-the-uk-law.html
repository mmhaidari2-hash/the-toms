Life in the UK test practice: Government and law (free questions with answers)

This is free practice made by New Life UK. It is not the official test or handbook and does not guarantee a pass. Study the official handbook and book the test only on GOV.UK.

The test has 24 questions, you have 45 minutes, and you need 75% to pass (at least 18 correct).

The UK is a parliamentary democracy with a constitutional monarch. The King is head of state, but the elected government runs the country. Parliament has two Houses: the elected House of Commons and the House of Lords, whose members are appointed.

There are six hundred and fifty Members of Parliament, one for each constituency. A general election must be held at least every five years. The Prime Minister leads the government and chooses the Cabinet, and the Speaker keeps order in the House of Commons.

Some powers are devolved. The Scottish Parliament sits in Edinburgh, the Senedd, the Welsh Parliament, sits in Cardiff, and the Northern Ireland Assembly sits at Stormont in Belfast.

Minor criminal cases go to a magistrates' court. Serious cases go to a Crown Court, where a jury of twelve people decides the verdict in England and Wales. Small money claims can be made online. Discrimination is against the law under the Equality Act 2010.

Key facts

  • Head of state: The King: a constitutional monarch
  • House of Commons: The elected House: 650 MPs
  • House of Lords: Members are appointed, not elected
  • The Speaker: Keeps order in the House of Commons; politically neutral
  • Holyrood: The Scottish Parliament, Edinburgh
  • The Senedd: The Welsh Parliament, Cardiff
  • Stormont: The Northern Ireland Assembly, Belfast
  • Crown Court: Serious criminal cases, with a jury
  • Magistrates' court: Minor criminal cases (England, Wales and Northern Ireland)
  • Equality Act 2010: Makes discrimination illegal

Practice questions

What type of government does the UK have?

  • A parliamentary democracy with a constitutional monarch
  • An absolute monarchy
  • A republic with a president
  • A one-party state
Answer

A parliamentary democracy with a constitutional monarch. The monarch is head of state, but Parliament makes the laws and the elected government runs the country.

Who is the head of state of the UK?

  • The King
  • The Prime Minister
  • The Speaker
  • The Lord Chancellor
Answer

The King. King Charles III is the head of state; the Prime Minister is the head of the government.

How many Members of Parliament (MPs) are there in the House of Commons?

  • 650
  • 500
  • 129
  • 1,000
Answer

650. There are 650 MPs, one for each constituency.

How often must a general election be held?

  • At least every five years
  • Every year
  • Every ten years
  • Whenever the King chooses
Answer

At least every five years. A general election must take place at least every five years.

Members of the House of Lords are elected by the public. True or false?

  • FALSE
  • TRUE
Answer

FALSE. Members of the House of Lords are not elected; most are appointed as life peers.

Who keeps order during debates in the House of Commons?

  • The Speaker
  • The Prime Minister
  • The King
  • The Leader of the Opposition
Answer

The Speaker. The Speaker chairs debates and must stay politically neutral.

Who chooses the members of the Cabinet?

  • The Prime Minister
  • The Speaker
  • The House of Lords
  • The voters directly
Answer

The Prime Minister. The Prime Minister appoints the Cabinet, a small group of senior ministers.

Which minister is responsible for the economy?

  • The Chancellor of the Exchequer
  • The Home Secretary
  • The Foreign Secretary
  • The Lord Chancellor
Answer

The Chancellor of the Exchequer. The Chancellor of the Exchequer is responsible for the economy and taxes.

Where does the Scottish Parliament sit?

  • Edinburgh
  • Glasgow
  • London
  • Aberdeen
Answer

Edinburgh. The Scottish Parliament meets at Holyrood in Edinburgh.

Where does the Senedd, the Welsh Parliament, meet?

  • Cardiff
  • Swansea
  • London
  • Bangor
Answer

Cardiff. The Senedd meets in Cardiff Bay.

What is the Northern Ireland Assembly often called, after the place where it meets?

  • Stormont
  • Holyrood
  • Westminster
  • Whitehall
Answer

Stormont. The Assembly meets at Parliament Buildings, Stormont, in Belfast.

How many members does the Scottish Parliament have?

  • 129
  • 60
  • 90
  • 650
Answer

129. The Scottish Parliament has 129 Members (MSPs).

Which court deals with serious criminal cases in England and Wales, with a jury?

  • The Crown Court
  • A magistrates' court
  • A youth court
  • The small claims court
Answer

The Crown Court. Serious criminal cases are tried at the Crown Court before a judge and jury.

How many people sit on a jury in a criminal trial in England and Wales?

  • 12
  • 6
  • 15
  • 20
Answer

12. A jury in England and Wales has 12 people. Scotland has its own rules.

How are people chosen for jury service?

  • At random from the electoral register
  • They volunteer online
  • Their employer chooses them
  • Only lawyers can be chosen
Answer

At random from the electoral register. Names are chosen at random from the electoral register.

Which law makes discrimination illegal in areas such as work and services?

  • The Equality Act 2010
  • Magna Carta
  • The Act of Union
  • The Bill of Rights 1689
Answer

The Equality Act 2010. The Equality Act 2010 protects people from discrimination because of things like age, disability, race, religion and sex.

In England and Wales, what is the minimum age to get married?

  • 18
  • 16
  • 21
  • 14
Answer

18. Since February 2023 the minimum age in England and Wales has been 18, even with parental consent. Forcing someone to marry is a crime.

Which international organisation does the UK belong to, with 56 member countries?

  • The Commonwealth
  • The European Union
  • The African Union
  • ASEAN
Answer

The Commonwealth. The Commonwealth is a voluntary association of 56 independent countries.

The UK left the European Union on 31 January 2020. True or false?

  • TRUE
  • FALSE
Answer

TRUE. The UK left the EU on 31 January 2020, after the 2016 referendum.

How do people vote in UK elections?

  • By secret ballot
  • By a show of hands
  • By telling a police officer
  • By signing a public list
Answer

By secret ballot. Voting is secret: nobody can make you say who you voted for.
