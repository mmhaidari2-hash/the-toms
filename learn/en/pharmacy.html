What to say at a UK pharmacy: asking the pharmacist in British English

You've had a sore throat for two days. Ask the pharmacist for advice.

What you need to do

  1. Describe your symptom
  2. Answer the safety question
  3. Ask how often to take it

The conversation

They say

Hello, how can I help?

Tip: Say what's wrong and for how long: 'I've had … for …'

  • Best reply
    Hi. I've had a sore throat for two days.
    Clear: what and how long.
  • Understood, but not natural
    My throat is bad.
    He'll understand, but say how long you've had it too.
  • Doesn't answer
    I need a prescription for a doctor.
    Just tell the pharmacist what the problem is.
They say

Are you taking any other medicines at the moment?

Tip: He asks about other medicines. Answer yes or no clearly.

  • Best reply
    No, I'm not taking anything else.
    A full, clear answer. Good.
  • Understood, but not natural
    No.
    Fine. A full sentence sounds more confident.
  • Doesn't answer
    Yes, two days.
    Listen again: he asks about other medicines, not how long.
They say

OK. These lozenges should help.

Tip: Ask 'How often should I …?'

  • Best reply
    How often should I take them?
    Exactly the right question.
  • Understood, but not natural
    How many?
    Understood, but 'How often should I take them?' is clearer.
  • Doesn't answer
    How much is the doctor?
    Ask about the lozenges, not the doctor. NHS GP visits are free anyway.
They say

It's on the box. Read the leaflet, and see your GP if it's not better in a week.

Tip: Thank him before you go.

  • Best reply
    Great, thank you for your help.
    Warm and polite.
  • Understood, but not natural
    OK, bye.
    Fine, but a thank you is warmer.
  • Doesn't answer
    Can I have antibiotics?
    Antibiotics need a prescription, and most sore throats don't need them.

How it ends

You are welcome. Feel better soon.
