Life in the UK test practice: History (free questions with answers)

This is free practice made by New Life UK. It is not the official test or handbook and does not guarantee a pass. Study the official handbook and book the test only on GOV.UK.

The test has 24 questions, you have 45 minutes, and you need 75% to pass (at least 18 correct).

People have lived in Britain for thousands of years. Stonehenge in Wiltshire was built in the Stone Age. The Romans invaded in AD 43 and stayed for about 400 years, and the Emperor Hadrian built a wall across the north of England.

In 1066 William of Normandy won the Battle of Hastings. In 1215 King John agreed to Magna Carta, which limited the king's power. The Black Death arrived in 1348 and killed a huge part of the population.

Henry the Eighth broke away from the Church of Rome and had six wives. Elizabeth the First's navy defeated the Spanish Armada in 1588. In 1707 England and Scotland were united as the Kingdom of Great Britain.

Many women over 30 won the vote in 1918, and women got the vote at the same age as men in 1928. Britain fought in two world wars. The NHS began in 1948, and Margaret Thatcher became the first woman Prime Minister in 1979.

Key facts

  • AD 43: The Romans invade Britain
  • 1066: Battle of Hastings: the Norman Conquest
  • 1215: Magna Carta limits the king's power
  • 1588: The Spanish Armada is defeated
  • 1707: England and Scotland unite as Great Britain
  • 1807: The slave trade is abolished in the British Empire
  • 1928: Women can vote at the same age as men
  • 1948: The NHS is founded
  • Boudicca: Queen of the Iceni who fought the Romans
  • Winston Churchill: Prime Minister during most of the Second World War

Practice questions

Where is Stonehenge?

  • Wiltshire
  • Cornwall
  • Kent
  • Yorkshire
Answer

Wiltshire. Stonehenge stands on Salisbury Plain in Wiltshire.

In which year did the Romans invade Britain?

  • AD 43
  • AD 410
  • 1066
  • 55 BC
Answer

AD 43. The Roman invasion began in AD 43.

Who was Boudicca?

  • A queen who fought against the Romans
  • A Roman emperor
  • A Norman knight
  • A Tudor princess
Answer

A queen who fought against the Romans. Boudicca, queen of the Iceni tribe, led a revolt against the Romans.

Which emperor built a wall across the north of England?

  • Hadrian
  • Julius Caesar
  • Augustus
  • Nero
Answer

Hadrian. Hadrian's Wall was built on the orders of the Emperor Hadrian.

Which battle in 1066 began the Norman Conquest?

  • The Battle of Hastings
  • The Battle of Bannockburn
  • The Battle of Waterloo
  • The Battle of Trafalgar
Answer

The Battle of Hastings. William of Normandy defeated King Harold at the Battle of Hastings.

What did Magna Carta, agreed in 1215, do?

  • It limited the power of the king
  • It created the NHS
  • It united England and Scotland
  • It gave women the vote
Answer

It limited the power of the king. Magna Carta established that even the king was subject to the law.

Which plague arrived in Britain in 1348?

  • The Black Death
  • Smallpox
  • Spanish flu
  • Cholera
Answer

The Black Death. The Black Death killed a large part of the population.

How many wives did Henry VIII have?

  • Six
  • Two
  • Four
  • Eight
Answer

Six. Henry VIII married six times.

Which fleet did the English navy defeat in 1588?

  • The Spanish Armada
  • The French navy at Trafalgar
  • The Viking fleet
  • The Dutch East India fleet
Answer

The Spanish Armada. The Spanish Armada was defeated during the reign of Elizabeth I.

Who wrote Romeo and Juliet and Hamlet?

  • William Shakespeare
  • Charles Dickens
  • Robert Burns
  • Jane Austen
Answer

William Shakespeare. William Shakespeare (1564 to 1616) was a playwright and poet.

In 1707, England and Scotland were united as the Kingdom of Great Britain. True or false?

  • TRUE
  • FALSE
Answer

TRUE. The Act of Union of 1707 created the Kingdom of Great Britain.

When was the slave trade abolished in the British Empire?

  • 1807
  • 1707
  • 1918
  • 1066
Answer

1807. Parliament passed an act to abolish the slave trade in 1807; slavery itself was abolished across the Empire in 1833.

Who led the British fleet at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805?

  • Admiral Nelson
  • The Duke of Wellington
  • Oliver Cromwell
  • Sir Francis Drake
Answer

Admiral Nelson. Admiral Nelson won at Trafalgar; his statue stands on Nelson's Column in Trafalgar Square.

Who defeated Napoleon at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815?

  • The Duke of Wellington
  • Admiral Nelson
  • Winston Churchill
  • King George III
Answer

The Duke of Wellington. The Duke of Wellington led the army that defeated Napoleon at Waterloo.

In 1918, which women were given the right to vote?

  • Women over 30
  • All women over 18
  • Only married women
  • Women over 21
Answer

Women over 30. In 1918 women over 30 who met a property qualification got the vote; in 1928 women could vote at 21, the same as men.

Who was Prime Minister for most of the Second World War?

  • Winston Churchill
  • Margaret Thatcher
  • Clement Attlee
  • David Lloyd George
Answer

Winston Churchill. Winston Churchill became Prime Minister in 1940 and led Britain through most of the war.

On what date did the D-Day landings in Normandy take place?

  • 6 June 1944
  • 11 November 1918
  • 1 September 1939
  • 8 May 1945
Answer

6 June 1944. Allied forces landed in Normandy on 6 June 1944.

In which year was the National Health Service (NHS) founded?

  • 1948
  • 1918
  • 1965
  • 1979
Answer

1948. The NHS began in 1948; Aneurin (Nye) Bevan was the minister who led it.

Who discovered penicillin?

  • Alexander Fleming
  • Isaac Newton
  • Charles Darwin
  • John Logie Baird
Answer

Alexander Fleming. Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.

Who was the first woman to become Prime Minister of the UK?

  • Margaret Thatcher
  • Theresa May
  • Emmeline Pankhurst
  • Elizabeth Fry
Answer

Margaret Thatcher. Margaret Thatcher became Prime Minister in 1979.

Who invented the World Wide Web?

  • Sir Tim Berners-Lee
  • John Logie Baird
  • Alan Turing
  • Frank Whittle
Answer

Sir Tim Berners-Lee. Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist, invented the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989; the first website went live in 1990.
