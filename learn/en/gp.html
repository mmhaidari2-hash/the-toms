How to book a GP appointment in the UK: phone phrases in British English

Your back has hurt for two weeks. Book an appointment at the GP surgery.

What you need to do

  1. Ask for an appointment
  2. Say what the problem is
  3. Accept a time

The conversation

They say

Good morning, how can I help?

Tip: Use 'I'd like to book an appointment'.

  • Best reply
    Hello, I'd like to book an appointment with a doctor, please.
    The standard phrase. Well done.
  • Understood, but not natural
    I want to see the doctor.
    Fine. 'I'd like to book an appointment' is the usual phrase.
  • Doesn't answer
    I'd like to register my car.
    That's not something the surgery does. Ask for an appointment.
They say

Can I ask what it's about?

Tip: Say the problem and how long you have had it.

  • Best reply
    I've had a bad back for about two weeks.
    Clear and useful for the receptionist.
  • Understood, but not natural
    My back.
    Understood. Saying how long helps them choose the right appointment.
  • Doesn't answer
    It's about two o'clock.
    She asked why you need the doctor, not the time.
They say

I can offer you Thursday at ten past nine, or a phone call tomorrow.

Tip: It's a choice. Say which one you want.

  • Best reply
    Thursday at ten past nine is fine, thanks.
    Clear. You repeated the time to check it.
  • Understood, but not natural
    Thursday.
    Fine. Repeating the time helps avoid mistakes.
  • Doesn't answer
    Yes.
    There are two options, so say which one.
They say

Lovely. Can I take your date of birth, please?

Tip: Say dates like 'the fourth of May, nineteen ninety'.

  • Best reply
    Yes, it's the fourth of May, nineteen ninety.
    The British way to say a date.
  • Understood, but not natural
    Four, five, ninety.
    She may write it down, but 'the fourth of May' is clearer.
  • Doesn't answer
    I'm thirty-six.
    She needs the date, not your age.

How it ends

Thank you. That's booked for Thursday at nine ten.
